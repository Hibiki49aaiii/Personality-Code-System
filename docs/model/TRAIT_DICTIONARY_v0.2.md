# Trait Dictionary v0.2

> Status: conceptual freeze for item authoring; not psychometrically validated
> Model family: PCS experimental
> Date: 2026-08-26

## 0. Purpose and rules

This dictionary defines the constructs PCS may directly measure in the v0.2 item-bank phase. A trait is a **continuous tendency**, not a moral rank, diagnosis, ability certificate, or permanent identity.

Rules:

- High and low scores must both describe coherent styles; neither side is automatically "better".
- Score anchors are behavioral prototypes, not percentile claims.
- A score must never be inferred from another trait merely because the traits are correlated.
- Presentation domains (Love, Work, Stress, etc.) are views composed from traits; they are not additional latent traits.
- The public result may use friendlier Japanese labels, but stable IDs never change within a published assessment-model version.

## SYS — Systems Modeling / 構造化思考

**Definition:** Tendency to represent situations as interacting components, dependencies, feedback loops, constraints, and causal relationships rather than as isolated events.

**Low-score pole:** Local/direct processing; responds to the immediate case without needing a larger system model.

**Not:** General intelligence, technical knowledge, abstraction alone, or perfectionism.

**Extreme-high failure mode:** Over-structuring simple or human situations; building a model when direct action or empathy would be enough.

**Extreme-low failure mode:** Treating repeated symptoms as separate incidents and missing upstream dependencies.

**Anchors:**
- **10:** Usually handles issues one at a time; rarely maps downstream effects unless prompted.
- **30:** Notices obvious dependencies but prefers concrete fixes over architecture.
- **50:** Switches between local problem solving and system views depending on stakes.
- **70:** Regularly maps causes, ownership, interfaces, and second-order effects before changing something.
- **90:** Automatically reconstructs the system behind events and may resist acting until the structure makes sense.

**Observable indicators:** dependency mapping, causal diagrams, upstream/downstream questions, interface/ownership analysis.

**Nearest overlaps:** ABS, CRE, ADV. **Boundary:** SYS concerns relations among parts; ABS concerns level of generalization; CRE concerns novel recombination; ADV concerns failure-search orientation.

**Domains:** Thinking, Work, Decision Making, Learning, Hidden Strengths.

**Item cautions:** Avoid technical/software vocabulary and avoid rewarding "strategic" self-image.

---

## VER — Evidence Verification / 検証志向

**Definition:** Tendency to require, seek, and cross-check evidence before accepting consequential claims or acting on them.

**Low-score pole:** Pragmatic acceptance; greater willingness to rely on trusted sources, convention, or plausible explanation without independent checking.

**Not:** Cynicism, paranoia, intelligence, or ADV.

**Extreme-high failure mode:** Excessive checking, high trust cost, repeated confirmation after decision-quality gains are negligible.

**Extreme-low failure mode:** Accepting consequential claims too readily or failing to verify changed assumptions.

**Anchors:**
- **10:** Usually accepts a plausible explanation from a trusted source without checking it independently.
- **30:** Verifies mainly when stakes are clearly high or something looks wrong.
- **50:** Balances source trust, stakes, and verification cost.
- **70:** Independently checks important claims and rechecks when evidence conflicts.
- **90:** Treats important claims as provisional until corroborated and may continue verifying after a practical decision is available.

**Observable indicators:** source checking, primary-source preference, re-verification after contradictions, evidence requests.

**Nearest overlaps:** ADV, CON, UNC. **Boundary:** VER asks "is this supported?"; ADV asks "how can this fail/be exploited?"; CON detects mismatch; UNC concerns functioning before certainty.

**Domains:** Thinking, Decision Making, Work, Risk, Adversarial Analysis.

**Item cautions:** Use both interpersonal and non-interpersonal situations; do not make verification sound obviously virtuous.

## ADV — Adversarial Scanning / 敵対的検証

**Definition:** Tendency to proactively search for failure modes, exploit paths, contradictions, hidden incentives, and ways a system or claim could break under hostile or unfavorable conditions.

**Low-score pole:** Cooperative/default-benign interpretation; focuses first on intended operation and ordinary use.

**Not:** Hostility, aggression, distrust of everyone, or VER.

**Extreme-high failure mode:** Treating ordinary people or benign situations as if they require security auditing; suspicion crowds out cooperation.

**Extreme-low failure mode:** Designing only for normal use and overlooking foreseeable abuse or failure.

**Anchors:**
- **10:** Rarely considers abuse/failure unless evidence of a problem already exists.
- **30:** Checks obvious risks but mostly reasons from intended behavior.
- **50:** Uses adversarial thinking when stakes or context justify it.
- **70:** Routinely asks what breaks, who benefits, and how rules can be circumvented.
- **90:** Automatically red-teams systems and arguments, including interpersonal claims, even when that level of scrutiny may be unnecessary.

**Observable indicators:** threat modeling, edge-case search, incentive analysis, contradiction attacks.

**Nearest overlaps:** VER, CON, SYS. **Boundary:** ADV is orientation toward hostile/failure cases; VER is evidence threshold; CON is mismatch sensitivity; SYS is structure mapping without necessary threat focus.

**Domains:** Thinking, Risk, Work, Adversarial Analysis, Decision Making.

**Item cautions:** Avoid cybersecurity jargon; include ordinary planning and rules scenarios.

---

## ABS — Abstraction Preference / 抽象化志向

**Definition:** Preference for extracting general principles, categories, and reusable models from specific cases rather than keeping knowledge tightly bound to the original context.

**Low-score pole:** Concrete/contextual processing; preserves situational detail and case-specific meaning.

**Not:** SYS, intelligence, imagination, or lack of practicality.

**Extreme-high failure mode:** Compressing away decisive local context and forcing cases into an elegant but inaccurate general model.

**Extreme-low failure mode:** Relearning similar lessons repeatedly because common structure is not extracted.

**Anchors:**
- **10:** Prefers examples and concrete cases; generalized models feel less useful than situational detail.
- **30:** Generalizes when similarities are obvious but usually stays close to examples.
- **50:** Uses abstraction or concreteness according to task demands.
- **70:** Habitually extracts reusable principles from multiple cases.
- **90:** Rapidly compresses experiences into general models and may underweight exceptions/context.

**Observable indicators:** analogies, principles, category formation, reusable mental models.

**Nearest overlaps:** SYS, CRE, META. **Boundary:** ABS is level of representation; SYS is relation/dependency modeling; CRE is novel combination; META is monitoring one's own cognition.

**Domains:** Thinking, Learning, Creativity, Hidden Strengths.

**Item cautions:** Avoid academic-status cues and math/science examples only.

---

## META — Metacognitive Monitoring / メタ認知

**Definition:** Tendency to notice, inspect, and revise one's own reasoning, assumptions, confidence, and bias while thinking or after a decision.

**Low-score pole:** Immersed/direct cognition; acts from judgments without frequently stepping outside the thought process to inspect it.

**Not:** Self-esteem, rumination, self-criticism, or COG.

**Extreme-high failure mode:** Recursive self-analysis replaces action or creates unnecessary doubt about ordinary decisions.

**Extreme-low failure mode:** Repeating reasoning errors without noticing how the conclusion was produced.

**Anchors:**
- **10:** Rarely asks how personal assumptions shaped a conclusion.
- **30:** Reflects mainly after visible mistakes or disagreement.
- **50:** Can inspect reasoning when stakes justify it without constantly self-monitoring.
- **70:** Regularly checks assumptions, confidence, and bias during important decisions.
- **90:** Continuously observes own cognition and may spend excessive time analyzing the analysis itself.

**Observable indicators:** confidence calibration language, assumption checks, postmortems, bias self-audit.

**Nearest overlaps:** COG, ABS, VER. **Boundary:** META targets one's reasoning process; COG targets emotion processing; VER targets external/internal claim evidence.

**Domains:** Thinking, Learning, Decision Making, Growth, Adversarial Analysis.

**Item cautions:** Do not equate self-doubt with metacognition.

---

## EMO — Emotional Access / 感情アクセス

**Definition:** Tendency/capacity to notice, differentiate, and directly recognize one's own primary emotional states.

**Low-score pole:** Lower immediate access/differentiation; emotions may be noticed later, through bodily cues, behavior, or analysis.

**Not:** Emotional intensity, empathy, sentimentality, mental health, or emotional regulation.

**Extreme-high failure mode:** If regulation is weak, immediate affect can dominate attention; high access itself is not pathology.

**Extreme-low failure mode:** Important emotional signals are recognized late or mislabeled as purely practical problems.

**Anchors:**
- **10:** Often realizes what was felt only well after the event, if at all.
- **30:** Can identify broad states such as anger/sadness but finer distinctions arrive slowly.
- **50:** Usually knows what is being felt when it matters, with some delayed cases.
- **70:** Notices and distinguishes emotions relatively quickly and can state them directly.
- **90:** Has very immediate, fine-grained access to changing emotional states.

**Observable indicators:** emotion differentiation, timing of recognition, ability to name primary feeling before explanation.

**Nearest overlaps:** COG, RDP, STR. **Boundary:** EMO is access/awareness, not how emotion is analyzed (COG), invested (RDP), or recovered from (STR).

**Domains:** Emotion, Relationships/Love, Stress, Communication, Growth.

**Item cautions:** Avoid "I am emotional" wording; separate awareness from intensity.

---

## COG — Cognitive Mediation of Emotion / 感情の認知処理

**Definition:** Tendency to process emotional experiences through language, causal explanation, categorization, and problem analysis while or before fully experiencing the affect.

**Low-score pole:** Experiential/embodied processing; allows feeling to unfold without immediately converting it into an explanatory model.

**Not:** Emotional intelligence, META, intelligence, or emotional suppression.

**Extreme-high failure mode:** Intellectualization: a correct explanation substitutes for acknowledging the underlying emotion.

**Extreme-low failure mode:** Emotional experience may remain difficult to organize, communicate, or translate into actionable understanding.

**Anchors:**
- **10:** Usually experiences emotion first and explains it much later, if explanation is needed.
- **30:** Uses analysis after emotion settles rather than during the experience.
- **50:** Can alternate between direct feeling and analytical processing.
- **70:** Quickly asks why the emotion occurred, what it means, and what should change.
- **90:** Almost automatically translates emotion into causes, structure, and solutions, sometimes before naming the primary feeling.

**Observable indicators:** causal analysis during conflict, problem-definition language, explanation preceding feeling statements.

**Nearest overlaps:** EMO, META, SYS. **Boundary:** COG is specifically the processing route for affect; META monitors cognition generally; SYS structures external/internal problems broadly.

**Domains:** Emotion, Relationships/Love, Stress, Communication.

**Item cautions:** High COG must not be written as more mature; low COG must not be written as irrational.

---

## BND — Boundary Assertion / 境界設定

**Definition:** Tendency to distinguish one's own responsibilities, time, limits, and consent from other people's expectations, and to communicate/enforce that distinction.

**Low-score pole:** Porous/accommodating boundaries; greater willingness to absorb others' expectations or adjust personal limits for connection/coordination.

**Not:** Empathy, selfishness, independence competence, or AUT.

**Extreme-high failure mode:** Normal requests, dependence, or coordination may be experienced as intrusion; boundaries become rigidity.

**Extreme-low failure mode:** Overcommitment, resentment, unclear responsibility, or difficulty refusing unwanted obligations.

**Anchors:**
- **10:** Frequently accepts expectations to preserve harmony even when personal limits are exceeded.
- **30:** Can refuse major demands but often accommodates smaller unwanted obligations.
- **50:** Negotiates limits according to context and relationship.
- **70:** Clearly distinguishes voluntary support from obligation and communicates limits early.
- **90:** Strongly protects personal scope and may reject ambiguous expectations before exploring compromise.

**Observable indicators:** refusal comfort, role clarity, consent language, reaction to implicit obligations.

**Nearest overlaps:** AUT, RDP, REC. **Boundary:** BND is limit/obligation separation; AUT is desire for self-direction; RDP is bond depth; REC is mutuality monitoring.

**Domains:** Relationships/Love, Work, Communication, Stress, Growth.

**Item cautions:** Include both work and personal contexts; avoid moralizing "healthy boundaries" language.

---

## RDP — Relational Depth / 関係深度

**Definition:** Preference for concentrating emotional/cognitive investment into a smaller number of deep, high-information relationships rather than maintaining many lighter connections.

**Low-score pole:** Breadth-oriented relational style; values wider networks, lighter ties, or lower-intensity connection.

**Not:** Introversion/extraversion, loyalty, dependency, attachment security, or social skill.

**Extreme-high failure mode:** Overinvestment in a few relationships can amplify loss, conflict, or role entanglement.

**Extreme-low failure mode:** Close partners may experience insufficient depth or exclusivity if their needs differ.

**Anchors:**
- **10:** Prefers many low-intensity connections and rarely seeks highly exclusive emotional depth.
- **30:** Has some close ties but does not need extensive private/shared inner-world depth.
- **50:** Comfortable with both deep and lighter relationships.
- **70:** Prefers a small circle and substantial mutual disclosure/shared history.
- **90:** Treats a few relationships as highly significant internal alliances and invests heavily in their meaning.

**Observable indicators:** selective disclosure, investment concentration, shared private context, preference for few close bonds.

**Nearest overlaps:** REC, BND, EMO. **Boundary:** RDP is investment depth; REC is symmetry monitoring; BND is fusion/limit management; EMO is own emotion access.

**Domains:** Relationships/Love, Communication, Stress.

**Item cautions:** Do not use popularity or number of friends as direct score proxy.

---

## REC — Reciprocity Sensitivity / 相互性感度

**Definition:** Tendency to notice and assign meaning to asymmetry in effort, responsiveness, prioritization, disclosure, and relational investment.

**Low-score pole:** Lower monitoring of symmetry; greater tolerance for temporary or chronic imbalance without interpreting it as relationship information.

**Not:** Generosity, scorekeeping behavior, fairness ideology, or RDP.

**Extreme-high failure mode:** Affection becomes accounting; ordinary fluctuations are treated as evidence of reduced commitment.

**Extreme-low failure mode:** One-sided relationships can persist longer because asymmetry carries little diagnostic weight.

**Anchors:**
- **10:** Rarely tracks who initiates, gives, or prioritizes more unless imbalance is extreme.
- **30:** Notices large asymmetry but usually does not attach much meaning to smaller differences.
- **50:** Uses reciprocity as one relationship signal among several.
- **70:** Regularly notices changes in mutual effort and interprets sustained imbalance as meaningful.
- **90:** Highly sensitive to relative effort/priority and may continuously monitor whether the relationship is mutually chosen.

**Observable indicators:** initiation balance, support asymmetry, response priority, mutual investment language.

**Nearest overlaps:** RDP, CON, BND. **Boundary:** REC concerns relative contribution/selection; CON concerns contradiction; RDP concerns depth; BND concerns limits.

**Domains:** Relationships/Love, Communication, Adversarial Analysis.

**Item cautions:** Avoid phrasing that makes low sensitivity sound exploitable or high sensitivity sound petty.

---

## CON — Consistency Sensitivity / 一貫性感度

**Definition:** Tendency to detect and be bothered by incongruence among a person's/system's words, actions, stated rules, and behavior across time.

**Low-score pole:** Contextual tolerance; greater willingness to treat inconsistency as normal adaptation, mood, exception, or situational variation.

**Not:** Honesty, memory, VER, or ADV.

**Extreme-high failure mode:** Normal human variability is treated as a defect requiring explanation; old statements can become permanent audit records.

**Extreme-low failure mode:** Repeated contradictions may not meaningfully update trust or expectations.

**Anchors:**
- **10:** Easily accepts that statements and behavior change with context without requiring reconciliation.
- **30:** Notices major contradictions but often lets minor ones pass.
- **50:** Distinguishes meaningful inconsistency from ordinary context changes.
- **70:** Remembers and evaluates mismatches across words/actions/time.
- **90:** Strongly requires contradictions to be explained and may revisit earlier events when a new inconsistency appears.

**Observable indicators:** timeline comparison, rule consistency checks, behavioral congruence questions.

**Nearest overlaps:** VER, ADV, REC, UNC. **Boundary:** CON detects mismatch; VER evaluates support; ADV searches hostile/failure possibilities; REC compares mutual investment; UNC tolerates incomplete explanation.

**Domains:** Relationships/Love, Thinking, Communication, Work, Stress.

**Item cautions:** Include self/organizational inconsistency, not only partner dishonesty scenarios.

---

## AUT — Autonomy Need / 自律性要求

**Definition:** Need for self-directed choice, discretion, ownership, and freedom to determine methods/goals without unnecessary external control.

**Low-score pole:** Comfort with externally defined structure, shared direction, established procedures, and coordinated decision authority.

**Not:** Competence, rebelliousness, BND, leadership, or independence of living.

**Extreme-high failure mode:** Necessary coordination or legitimate authority is experienced as needless control; resistance becomes reflexive.

**Extreme-low failure mode:** Overreliance on external direction and reduced initiative when structure disappears.

**Anchors:**
- **10:** Prefers clear external direction and feels little need to redesign the decision structure.
- **30:** Comfortable with rules and supervision when expectations are clear.
- **50:** Values discretion but accepts structure when it improves coordination.
- **70:** Strongly prefers ownership over methods and resists arbitrary restrictions.
- **90:** Needs substantial self-governance and may disengage from environments with little control even when other conditions are favorable.

**Observable indicators:** reaction to micromanagement, method ownership, self-directed planning, rule challenge.

**Nearest overlaps:** BND, EXE, OPT. **Boundary:** AUT is desire for control over choices; BND is limit enforcement; EXE is starting action; OPT is improving systems.

**Domains:** Work, Decision Making, Relationships/Love, Leadership-derived profile, Growth.

**Item cautions:** Avoid entrepreneurial prestige cues.

---

## EXE — Action Initiation / 行動開始性

**Definition:** Tendency to transition from sufficient understanding/intention into concrete action without excessive additional deliberation.

**Low-score pole:** Deliberative delay; prefers additional preparation, certainty, or planning before initiating.

**Not:** Impulsivity, persistence, productivity, risk appetite, or finishing.

**Extreme-high failure mode:** Action can begin before the model/plan is mature enough, especially when paired with low VER.

**Extreme-low failure mode:** Analysis/preparation continues after the expected value of more deliberation has fallen.

**Anchors:**
- **10:** Frequently remains in planning/research after a viable first step is available.
- **30:** Starts once uncertainty is low and preparation feels complete.
- **50:** Balances preparation and action based on reversibility/stakes.
- **70:** Moves quickly once a workable path exists and refines through iteration.
- **90:** Strong bias toward execution; may start experiments or implementation while others are still defining the problem.

**Observable indicators:** latency from decision to first action, prototype behavior, iteration preference.

**Nearest overlaps:** FIN, PER, UNC, RSK. **Boundary:** EXE is starting; FIN is closing; PER is continuing; UNC is comfort with missing information; RSK is downside acceptance.

**Domains:** Action, Work, Decision Making, Leadership-derived profile.

**Item cautions:** Separate reversible experiments from reckless action.

---

## OPT — Optimization Drive / 最適化欲求

**Definition:** Tendency to keep searching for efficiency, quality, structure, or performance improvements after a system/output is already functional.

**Low-score pole:** Satisficing; accepts a solution once it meets the required threshold and redirects attention elsewhere.

**Not:** Perfectionism as clinical construct, SYS, conscientiousness, or FIN.

**Extreme-high failure mode:** Endless improvement loops, moving requirements, diminishing-return work, or unnecessary redesign.

**Extreme-low failure mode:** Leaves significant easy improvements unrealized and may tolerate inefficient routines.

**Anchors:**
- **10:** Stops improving once basic requirements are met unless a clear problem appears.
- **30:** Makes obvious improvements but rarely pursues marginal gains.
- **50:** Optimizes when benefit justifies cost.
- **70:** Regularly notices improvement opportunities and revisits working systems.
- **90:** Finds it difficult to leave visible inefficiency or quality headroom untouched, even when "done" is operationally sufficient.

**Observable indicators:** refactoring, process improvement, repeated revisions, efficiency comparisons.

**Nearest overlaps:** FIN, SYS, NOV. **Boundary:** OPT seeks better state; FIN defines stopping; SYS models structure; NOV seeks newness rather than improvement per se.

**Domains:** Action, Work, Growth, Adversarial Analysis.

**Item cautions:** Include cases where optimization has opportunity cost.

---

## FIN — Closure Discipline / 完了規律

**Definition:** Tendency to define completion criteria, stop further refinement, ship/close an output, and clear open loops.

**Low-score pole:** Open-loop orientation; work remains extendable, revisable, or partially completed without strong closure pressure.

**Not:** Persistence, quality, speed, or OPT.

**Extreme-high failure mode:** Premature closure or rigid adherence to "done" despite meaningful new evidence.

**Extreme-low failure mode:** Many nearly-finished projects, unclear stopping conditions, and accumulating maintenance/open loops.

**Anchors:**
- **10:** Frequently leaves work in an improvable near-complete state without a firm ship/close point.
- **30:** Completes externally required work but personal/internal projects often remain open.
- **50:** Usually closes work while allowing justified iteration.
- **70:** Sets explicit stopping criteria and protects shipping from unnecessary expansion.
- **90:** Strong closure discipline; will cut scope or reject late improvements to preserve completion.

**Observable indicators:** definition-of-done, scope cuts, shipping, archive/close behavior.

**Nearest overlaps:** OPT, PER, EXE. **Boundary:** FIN is stopping/closing; OPT is improving; PER is continuing effort; EXE is starting.

**Domains:** Action, Work, Growth, Adversarial Analysis.

**Item cautions:** Distinguish "finished" from "high quality" and externally forced deadlines.

---

## NOV — Novelty Seeking / 新奇性志向

**Definition:** Attraction toward new information, changing environments, unfamiliar domains, new projects, or unexplored possibilities.

**Low-score pole:** Familiarity/stability preference; gains value from depth, routine, predictability, and refinement of known areas.

**Not:** Creativity, impulsivity, openness as a whole, or lack of persistence.

**Extreme-high failure mode:** Project proliferation, stimulation chasing, premature switching after the model becomes familiar.

**Extreme-low failure mode:** Avoids beneficial exploration or adapts slowly when the environment changes.

**Anchors:**
- **10:** Strongly prefers known routines/domains and seldom seeks novelty for its own sake.
- **30:** Enjoys occasional change but generally prefers stable depth.
- **50:** Mixes exploration and familiarity according to opportunity.
- **70:** Regularly seeks new domains, projects, experiences, or methods.
- **90:** Rapidly orients toward unfamiliar/high-information opportunities and may lose interest once novelty collapses.

**Observable indicators:** new-project frequency, domain switching, exploration behavior, boredom with repetition.

**Nearest overlaps:** CRE, OPT, PER, RSK. **Boundary:** NOV seeks newness; CRE recombines ideas; OPT improves current systems; PER sustains effort; RSK concerns downside acceptance.

**Domains:** Action, Learning, Creativity, Relationships/Love, Work.

**Item cautions:** Avoid equating travel/social activity with novelty.

---

## PER — Persistence / 継続性

**Definition:** Tendency to sustain effort on a chosen objective after novelty, immediate reward, or emotional momentum declines.

**Low-score pole:** Interest-contingent continuation; effort drops more readily when stimulation/reward fades or obstacles persist.

**Not:** FIN, grit as a global virtue, stubbornness, or EXE.

**Extreme-high failure mode:** Sunk-cost persistence and difficulty abandoning objectives that should be stopped.

**Extreme-low failure mode:** Valuable projects are abandoned after the stimulating early phase.

**Anchors:**
- **10:** Effort usually falls sharply once novelty/reward disappears.
- **30:** Continues when deadlines/accountability are strong but personal momentum is fragile.
- **50:** Persists when goals remain worthwhile and disengages when evidence changes.
- **70:** Maintains effort through boring middle phases and temporary setbacks.
- **90:** Can sustain long effort with little immediate reinforcement and may need explicit stop rules to avoid sunk cost.

**Observable indicators:** middle-phase continuation, routine maintenance, response to slow progress.

**Nearest overlaps:** FIN, EXE, NOV, OPT. **Boundary:** PER is continuing; FIN is closing; EXE is starting; NOV predicts attraction to newness; OPT predicts improvement seeking.

**Domains:** Action, Work, Learning, Growth.

**Item cautions:** Ask about voluntary goals as well as externally enforced tasks.

---

## RSK — Risk Appetite / リスク許容志向

**Definition:** Willingness to accept meaningful downside variance, loss probability, or volatility in exchange for potential upside.

**Low-score pole:** Downside-conservative preference; favors lower variance, protection, and preservation even when upside is reduced.

**Not:** Risk-calibration skill, bravery, UNC, impulsivity, or financial behavior specifically.

**Extreme-high failure mode:** Accepts more downside than objectives/resources justify, especially when understanding creates false control confidence.

**Extreme-low failure mode:** Foregoes positive expected-value opportunities because downside possibility dominates.

**Anchors:**
- **10:** Strongly prefers preserving a secure outcome over materially uncertain upside.
- **30:** Accepts risk mainly when downside is tightly bounded or recovery is easy.
- **50:** Adjusts risk acceptance to stakes, upside, and recoverability.
- **70:** Willingly accepts meaningful uncertainty/downside when expected upside is attractive.
- **90:** Comfortable placing substantial resources/outcomes at risk for asymmetric upside and may underweight tail loss without guardrails.

**Observable indicators:** variance preference, downside acceptance, optionality tradeoffs, willingness to expose resources.

**Nearest overlaps:** UNC, NOV, EXE. **Boundary:** RSK concerns downside/variance; UNC concerns missing information; NOV concerns newness; EXE concerns initiation speed.

**Domains:** Risk, Decision Making, Work, Adversarial Analysis.

**Item cautions:** Use several domains; do not infer from crypto, gambling, entrepreneurship, or sport alone.

---

## UNC — Uncertainty Tolerance / 不確実性耐性

**Definition:** Ability/tendency to continue thinking, deciding, and functioning when relevant information, outcomes, or explanations remain incomplete.

**Low-score pole:** Need for closure/control; discomfort rises until ambiguity is reduced or a coherent explanation is available.

**Not:** Risk appetite, VER, anxiety diagnosis, or lack of planning.

**Extreme-high failure mode:** May normalize unresolved ambiguity that actually deserves investigation.

**Extreme-low failure mode:** Small unresolved uncertainties consume disproportionate attention or delay decisions.

**Anchors:**
- **10:** Strongly uncomfortable acting or resting while important questions remain unresolved.
- **30:** Can tolerate short ambiguity but seeks closure before committing.
- **50:** Functions with uncertainty while reducing it when decision value warrants.
- **70:** Comfortable making provisional decisions and revising as information arrives.
- **90:** Can operate for long periods with unresolved questions and may need reminders that some uncertainty is actionable risk.

**Observable indicators:** provisional decisions, reaction to incomplete explanations, ambiguity tolerance, closure-seeking.

**Nearest overlaps:** VER, CON, RSK, EXE, STR. **Boundary:** UNC is functioning under incomplete knowledge; VER is evidence threshold; CON is contradiction sensitivity; RSK is downside acceptance; STR is recovery after stress.

**Domains:** Stress, Decision Making, Risk, Work, Relationships/Love.

**Item cautions:** Do not phrase low UNC as "anxious"; uncertainty can be cognitively irritating without clinical anxiety.

---

## STR — Stress Recovery / ストレス回復性

**Definition:** Tendency to return toward one's ordinary cognitive/emotional baseline after a stressor ends or becomes controlled.

**Low-score pole:** Stress carryover; activation and cognitive/emotional effects persist after the immediate problem has passed.

**Not:** Stress exposure, toughness, emotional suppression, pain tolerance, or UNC.

**Extreme-high failure mode:** Apparent rapid return can hide under-processing, but PCS must not infer suppression from STR alone.

**Extreme-low failure mode:** Accumulated carryover reduces available attention and recovery between events.

**Anchors:**
- **10:** Stress effects often remain active long after the triggering event ends.
- **30:** Needs substantial decompression and may carry problems into the next context/day.
- **50:** Recovery speed varies with severity and control.
- **70:** Usually returns to baseline relatively quickly after the situation stabilizes.
- **90:** Strongly compartmentalizes/rebounds after pressure, while separate traits determine whether emotions were processed.

**Observable indicators:** recovery latency, sleep/attention carryover (non-clinical wording), ability to disengage after resolution.

**Nearest overlaps:** UNC, EMO, COG. **Boundary:** STR is recovery after activation; UNC concerns ambiguity during unresolved situations; EMO/COG concern emotional processing.

**Domains:** Stress, Work, Relationships/Love, Growth.

**Item cautions:** Avoid medical/trauma claims; do not equate "I keep working" with recovered baseline.

## CRE — Recombinative Creativity / 再結合創造性

**Definition:** Tendency to generate novel, potentially useful configurations by combining concepts, methods, or patterns that originated in different contexts.

**Low-score pole:** Conventional refinement/application; prefers proven combinations and deepening within established frames.

**Not:** Artistic skill, intelligence, ABS, novelty seeking, or actual innovation success.

**Extreme-high failure mode:** Produces many clever combinations without enough validation, feasibility, or completion.

**Extreme-low failure mode:** Misses opportunities that require importing structure from outside the familiar domain.

**Anchors:**
- **10:** Usually improves or applies known approaches rather than combining distant ideas.
- **30:** Makes novel combinations when similarities are obvious.
- **50:** Can recombine ideas when a problem calls for it but does not constantly seek unusual connections.
- **70:** Frequently imports mechanisms/ideas from other contexts and tests new combinations.
- **90:** Rapidly generates cross-context combinations and may create more plausible directions than can realistically be validated or finished.

**Observable indicators:** mechanism transfer, cross-domain analogies used to create options, hybrid solutions.

**Nearest overlaps:** ABS, SYS, NOV. **Boundary:** CRE generates new combinations; ABS generalizes; SYS maps relations; NOV seeks unfamiliarity.

**Domains:** Creativity, Learning, Hidden Strengths, Work, Adversarial Analysis.

**Item cautions:** Measure behavior tendency, not self-label "creative person".

## 2. Removed from direct trait set in v0.2

### LDR — Leadership Structure

**Decision:** Remove as independent measured trait.

**Reason:** Too context-dependent and substantially composed of AUT, BND, EXE, CON, OPT, plus competence/role opportunity not measured by PCS. Keep as a **derived presentation profile** only after interaction rules are defined.

### DEL — Delegation

**Decision:** Remove as independent measured trait.

**Reason:** Delegation depends on role authority, team quality, stakes, trust, VER, AUT, CON, and BND. A direct global score risks measuring job circumstance rather than personality. Keep as a **derived work behavior hypothesis**, not a numeric trait.

### TRN — Cross-domain Transfer

**Decision:** Remove as independent measured trait.

**Reason:** It overlaps heavily with ABS + CRE + SYS and may be closer to demonstrated cognitive performance than a self-report personality tendency. Keep as a **derived hidden-strength profile** until validated with behavioral tasks or external criteria.

## 4. v0.2 freeze rule

The 21 retained constructs are frozen **only for candidate item authoring**.
